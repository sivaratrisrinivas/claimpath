# Why this problem is real (full evidence)

Back to the [README](../README.md).

## Why

A rejection letter is written to end the conversation. It is short, confident, and cites one clause. Most people read it, feel it is final, and stop. In the US, KFF found that fewer than 1% of denied in-network marketplace claims were appealed in 2023. In India, the Insurance Ombudsman received 31,490 health complaints in FY2023-24, almost all about rejected claims (both checked at primary sources; see the table below).

The frustrating part: the rules are often on the policyholder's side. India's regulator says that after 60 months of continuous cover a claim cannot be contested for non-disclosure. US federal rules say emergency services cannot require prior authorization. But those rules are scattered across circulars and CFR sections nobody reads, and the insurer's letter never mentions them.

The rules that make a rejection contestable are cited on every rule node and listed under [Sources](sources.md): the [IRDAI Master Circular on Health Insurance Business, 29 May 2024](https://taxguru.in/corporate-law/master-circular-irdai-insurance-productsregulations-2024health-insurance.html) (60-month moratorium, item 13; rejection letters, item 17), the [Insurance Ombudsman Rules, 2017](https://www.cioins.co.in/Procedure/Index) (rule 14), and for the US the [ACA external review rules, 45 CFR 147.136](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-A/subchapter-B/part-147/section-147.136), the [ERISA claims procedure, 29 CFR 2560.503-1](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/subtitle-B/chapter-XXV/subchapter-F/part-2560/section-2560.503-1) and the [emergency-services rule, 45 CFR 149.110](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-A/subchapter-B/part-149/subpart-B/section-149.110). The 36-month PED cap remains confirmed only through [secondary reporting](https://www.cnbctv18.com/personal-finance/insurance-irdai-reduces-waiting-period-moratorium-lower-how-will-health-policies-benefit-19396754.htm).

These numbers show that rejected claims are common and rarely contested; they do not show how often a rejection is wrong.
